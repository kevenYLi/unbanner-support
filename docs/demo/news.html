A quiet comet is about to put on its brightest show in decades

By Mara Ellison · October 9
Illustration: The Daily Orbit

For most of the year it was little more than a smudge in the telescopes of a few dedicated observers. Now the comet is swinging toward the Sun, and astronomers expect it to brighten enough to be seen without binoculars for a few weeks this autumn.

The best views will come just before dawn, low in the eastern sky. Find a spot away from city lights, give your eyes twenty minutes to adjust, and look for a soft glow with a faint tail pointing away from the horizon.

“Comets are notoriously hard to predict,” said one observatory researcher. “But this one has behaved well so far, and the dust it is shedding should catch the sunlight nicely.”

If the weather does not cooperate, there is still time: the comet should remain visible until early November, fading slowly as it heads back out of the inner solar system.

Photographers planning to capture it should use a tripod, a wide lens and exposures of a few seconds. A phone on a stable surface in night mode can do surprisingly well.
